# Placedon — plan, September 2026

One consolidated document. Six analyses already exist in the backend repo
(competitor patterns, competitor feature matrix, India feature plan, agent
architecture, workflow backlog, product build plan); this is the synthesis they
do not contain, and it is written to be argued with rather than admired.

---

## 1. What this is

> A verified legal-evidence system for Indian corporate law. It uses existing
> language models as reasoning components, but controls their inputs, sources,
> temporal versions, outputs, citations and review states.
>
> **The model may propose. The system must verify. The reviewer decides.**

We are not training a model. That is settled and it is not ideological: the
monthly compute budget is ₹3,500, which is two to three orders of magnitude
below what post-training a legal model costs. The companies that own models
raised very large sums first and rented someone else's for years before that.

The honest one-line summary: **we are far ahead on everything underneath the
product and have not built the product.** A lawyer cannot use any of this today,
because there is no way to put a document in or get an answer out.

That asymmetry is the plan's central fact. The surfaces are ordinary software —
grids, uploads, routes — buildable in weeks. The verification core took months
and does not transfer from a contract-review product.

## 3. The wedge, and why it is defensible

The obvious competitors monetise **private document mass**: many contracts of
one type, or tens of thousands of documents in one matter, compared against a
firm's playbook. Every one of those assumptions fails for an Indian practice
without big-firm infrastructure. No document management system. No playbook. No
data room. Small volumes.

What that practice does have is a statutory compliance burden where the ground
truth is **public** — and unevenly commenced, amended in place, and defective at
source. We have logged four distinct transcription defects in the government's
own rendering.

So the inversion:

> In tabular contract review, a row is **a document you have**.
> In a compliance matrix, a row is **an obligation the law imposes** —
> and the most valuable rows are the ones with **no document behind them**.

A company that held no board meeting all year still gets a row saying so. A
document-mass architecture cannot generate that row, because a corpus can only
be asked about what is in it.

**Three things follow that are hard to copy:**

1. **Point-in-time law.** Contract clauses do not commence in stages by
   notification. Indian statutory obligations do. Answering "what did the law
   require on the date of this meeting" requires amendment chains and
   commencement provenance that a contract product has no reason to build.
2. **A deterministic critic.** Most agent stacks put a model in the reviewing
   seat, where it can hallucinate its own critique. Ours is pattern-matching
   code: reproducible, auditable, and unable to invent a justification. This is
   only possible because the ground truth is fixed public statute.
3. **Refusal that names what is missing.** Today the small-company
   classification *refuses* — because the operative threshold lives in a
   delegated rule we have not properly acquired — and it says which instrument,
   why, and what would unblock it.

## 4. What we are not doing

- Not training or post-training a model.
- Not building a Vault-scale document workspace; nobody in this market has
  100,000 documents.
- Not comparing against a firm playbook; there is no playbook to compare to.
- Not building contract review yet. It is a different product with different
  ground truth, and pursuing it now splits the wedge.
- Not building a clean-statute browser or Act download. Statutory text may only
  ever be served together with original matter.
- Not making any accuracy claim before §7 is satisfied.

## 5. Roadmap

Sequenced so that **each stage is a complete product on its own**, and so that
Stage 1 needs no API key at all — because Stage 1 is what every later stage
degrades to when the budget runs out.

### Stage 1 — Compliance matrix · no model · ₹0 per matter
`CompanyProfile` → obligation register → applicability → evidence card, and the
first Companies Act route this system has ever had. Retire the PoSH surface.
*Does not claim:* that any obligation was complied with. Only that it attaches,
does not attach, or cannot be determined.

### Stage 2 — Matter workspace · no model
One company, several financial years, documents attached to obligations. One
correction visibly invalidating the rows downstream of it.
*Does not claim:* extraction. Facts are typed in.

### Stage 3 — Tabular review, transposed · no model
Obligations × companies rather than documents × questions. Every cell links to
a source span and carries an export hash.

### Stage 4 — Extraction · first model call
The model reads uploaded documents and proposes typed facts. **Every extracted
value must be a verbatim span present in the document, or it is rejected.**
Roughly ₹0.38 per matter.

### Stage 5 — Narration and research with citations
The model turns an already-decided card into prose. It cannot change the answer,
the legal basis, or the verification status. Roughly ₹1.41 per matter fully
featured — about 2,400 matters a month inside the cap.

### Stage 6 — Review governance surfaced
Approval, audit history, superseded decisions, retraction. The machinery exists;
this makes it visible.

Four of six stages cost nothing per matter and carry the entire
differentiation.

## 6. What is blocked, and on whom

| Blocker | Blocks | Owner |
|---|---|---|
| G.S.R. 700(E) unacquired | small-company arithmetic, and every regime that turns on it | a person with a browser |
| Rules corpus: 1 of many | most operative thresholds | acquisition |
| No real-document benchmark | any accuracy statement | benchmark work |
| Zero lawyer review | every claim about usefulness | founder |
| No interview with a corporate lawyer or CS | the entire workflow ranking | founder |

Two of these are not engineering problems, and the second is the more serious.
**Not one of fourteen ranked workflows has an evidenced frequency.** Four
interviews, none with a corporate lawyer or a Company Secretary, no recorded
frequency, duration, or willingness to pay. Every frequency estimate in the
backlog is marked ASSUMED, and it should stay marked until someone asks.

There is also an unresolved segment question: the scope document locks
"lawyer, not CS", yet every well-evidenced workflow in the backlog is Company
Secretary work. That is a positioning decision, not a coding one.

---

## 7. What must be true before any accuracy claim

All four, not any of them:

1. A benchmark of real documents including deliberately defective ones, with
   ground truth recorded independently of the system.
2. Every benchmark bucket carrying both labels. Two of three currently do not,
   so two of three cannot measure what they claim to.
3. At least two practising corporate lawyers having reviewed real output.
4. False negatives measured. They never have been, because every document we
   hold is compliant.

Until then the honest statement is that the system is internally consistent and
refuses when it should — which is worth something, and is not accuracy.

---

## 8. Open questions

- **Segment.** Lawyer or Company Secretary? Every evidence signal points at CS;
  the scope document says lawyer.
- **Surface.** All four interviews describe documents arriving physically.
  Every document in our corpus is text-extractable. OCR is untested.
- **Willingness to pay.** No figure has ever been recorded. Any pricing tier
  proposed today is a guess, and third-party figures published about other
  vendors are unverified and should not be repeated as fact.
- **Acquisition ceiling.** If the operative thresholds for most obligations sit
  in rules we cannot reach, how much of the Act can this product actually
  answer? That number is unknown and it bounds the whole business.

---

## 9. The next three actions

1. Acquire G.S.R. 700(E) — one browser download and one command.
2. Ship Stage 1 — the compliance matrix behind one route, no model.
3. Put it in front of one practising Company Secretary and record what they say.

The third is the one that has been deferred longest and is worth the most. Both
companies this product is measured against started at a lawyer's desk. This one
has not been there yet.
