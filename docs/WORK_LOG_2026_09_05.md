## 1. "Bloomberg Law for India" — the four mechanics, tested one by one

The question was whether to fuse a Harvey-style legal AI with a live-data engine
("God's Eye View") to build an Indian Bloomberg Law. The body of that analysis lives
in the backend repo (`docs/BLOOMBERG_FOR_INDIA_ANALYSIS.md`); what follows is the new
half — decomposing what Bloomberg Law *actually does* and testing each mechanic
against Indian conditions.

| Bloomberg mechanic | How it works there | Indian verdict |
|---|---|---|
| **Draft Analyzer** — clause benchmarking | Semantic index over ~2.3M agreements filed to SEC EDGAR | **No data substrate — absent, not merely hard** |
| **Points of Law** — ML-extracted holdings + treatment | NLP over judicial opinions | Buildable, but off-wedge and needs model-authoritative labelling we refuse |
| **Docket analytics** — judge grant/deny rates | Structured PACER scrape | Off-wedge; legally fraught in India |
| **Corporate entity graph** — LEI-keyed hierarchy | Terminal infrastructure | **Transfers cleanly — and is already built** |

### The load-bearing finding

Bloomberg's highest-value corporate feature exists because **17 CFR 229.601(b)(10)
(Reg S-K Item 601) requires every material non-ordinary-course contract to be filed
as an exhibit.** That filing mandate is what creates a corpus.

India has no counterpart. SEBI LODR is a *particulars-disclosure* regime. SEBI Master
Circular SEBI/HO/CFD/CFD-PoD-1/P/CIR/2023/123 (13 Jul 2023), Annexure I, requires only:

> "significant terms of the agreement **(in brief)**"

Reg 30A extends the duty to the company's website — but to the same particulars list,
never the executed instrument.

**Consequence:** clause benchmarking is not a product that could be lost to a
better-funded rival. It is a product **nobody can build in India**, because the
corpus is not public. This reframes the wedge favourably: with no public contract
corpus, the statute is the only dense, public, authoritative corpus in Indian
corporate law — and that is the one Placedon is built on.

### Status: PARTIALLY_VERIFIED, with two corrections

The claim was carried as UNVERIFIED and then checked against the regulators' own
text rather than asserted. It holds. Two corrections came out of the check:

1. **Reg 30A is far broader than first drafted.** Not "shareholder/family agreements" —
   clause 5A reaches *any* agreement among shareholders, promoters, promoter-group
   entities, related parties, directors, KMP or employees (of the entity or its
   holding/subsidiary/associate) affecting management, control, restriction or
   liability, **including agreements the listed entity is not a party to**. This
   strengthens the conclusion: even that expansive duty stops at terms-in-brief.
2. **One genuine full-text exception exists.** MCA charge filings (CHG-1/CHG-9 under
   s.77) carry the charge instrument itself into the public record. Narrow —
   secured-lending instruments only; nothing commercial attaches to AOC-4 or MGT-7.
   A lender-security corpus cannot support clause benchmarking. Its **scale is left
   UNVERIFIED** rather than estimated, and NCLT scheme filings are recorded
   UNRESOLVED because they were not checked.

**Why PARTIALLY_VERIFIED and not VERIFIED:** the SEBI board memorandum was fetched
from sebi.gov.in (primary), but two anchors are primary-*adjacent* — the circular's
Annexure came from an exchange mirror rather than a sebi.gov.in-hosted PDF, and Item
601(b)(10) came from Cornell LII because ecfr.gov returned a bot-verification wall.
That wall was **recorded as blocked and not bypassed**, per the source policy. Both
are verbatim reproductions, neither is the regulator's own host.

### A correction to the received wisdom

The common claim that the **SCC citation monopoly** keeps foreign entrants out is no
longer true: Harvey signed SCC Online in January 2026. A foreign entrant has already
crossed that barrier — just not Bloomberg. So Bloomberg's absence from India is a
statement about Bloomberg's priorities, **not evidence of a moat**. Of the usual four
reasons given, only price sensitivity is durable, and it cuts against us too — which
is why the published flat-price position, rather than enterprise relationship sales,
remains right.

### On forking an open-source spatial engine

Technically possible, strategically near-zero for this wedge. Such a codebase is a
WebGL/Cesium client plus a telemetry proxy; none of it touches statute text, entity
resolution, provenance or entailment — the half of the problem that is actually hard.
It would also import a frontend stack into an engine whose stated virtue is no
dependencies outside the standard library. Live telemetry that matters here is
Gazette amendments and registry filings, not ships and planes. If a spatial product
is ever wanted, it is a separate bet with a separate buyer.

## 2. The validation kit was describing an engine that no longer exists

The kit is the one artifact a reviewing Company Secretary actually opens. It had
drifted from the engine after G.S.R. 700(E) was attested, and still claimed the
₹4 crore / ₹40 crore small-company figures were *"located but not properly acquired —
so we refuse the arithmetic."*

That was no longer true. The instrument is acquired, hash-recorded and attested, and
the arithmetic runs. The row does still refuse for that specimen company, but for a
different reason — the holding/subsidiary exclusion limb was not supplied, and an
untested limb is never assumed satisfied.

Sending a practitioner a page that misstates *why* the tool refuses would have burned
the single question the review exists to ask — "is the refusal useful or annoying?" —
on a cause that no longer applies, while understating the system.

Fixed, without redesign:
- the s.2(85) row now states the attested thresholds and the real blocker;
- the evidence-pack line names the exclusion limb, not the instrument;
- "Seven obligations" → thirteen, and the "rules we do not hold" example moved to
  s.177 and s.203, which are the rows that now refuse outright and name the exact
  rule blocking them;
- a claim of "all five outcome states" was corrected to four, which is what the
  matrix actually shows. In a document whose entire pitch is that the tool does not
  overstate what it knows, a count a reader can check in ten seconds had to be right.

## 3. Autonomous loop — two tasks, then an honest stop

Run in an isolated git worktree on `loop/register-wiring`, because a second Claude
session was committing to `engine/entailment` throughout and two autonomous writers
on one branch would have raced.

**Before building, the queue was verified against the code rather than the plan doc —
and two of three proposed tasks were already done:** s.185/186/188 were already
register rows, and the CIN mapping had shipped in the peer session's
`mca_aggregator.py`. ("DIN as identity" was also dropped: `entity_graph.py`
deliberately excludes DINs as PII.)

**A1 — s.180 and s.184 as company-level register rows.** Both had deciders but no
row, so a company that had uploaded nothing was never told these controls applied to
it. Rows come from the law, not from documents — a decider without a row is invisible
to exactly the company most likely to need it. Register 13 → 15.

Both refuse rather than accuse. An over-limit borrowing is UNDETERMINED, not a
defect, because exceeding the s.180(1)(c) aggregate is lawful with a special
resolution we cannot see. An interested director's contract is UNDETERMINED because
the duty is discharged *by* disclosing, which is a minute-book fact.

Worth recording: two tests written during this task asserted SATISFIED on an empty
tuple. The engine was right and the tests were wrong — `limbs_not_decided` correctly
blocks any "compliant" reading, because everything-we-can-check-passed is not
compliance with the whole provision. The tests were corrected to assert that the
undecided limbs are *named* rather than silently dropped.

**A2 — cross-section retrieval eval widened 45 → 70 cases**, reaching 62 sections
instead of 37. Every label was read off the section's own title in the corpus —
structural, not a legal judgement — which is why this was safe to do without a lawyer.

| Metric | 45 cases | 70 cases |
|---|---|---|
| precision@1 | 0.73 | **0.71** |
| recall@5 | 0.93 | **0.91** |

The near-identical numbers on a 56%-larger, harder set are the useful result: the
earlier score was not an artefact of 45 friendly cases. All six p@1 misses are one
class — semantic near-neighbours a lexical ranker cannot separate (s.14→467,
s.169→242, s.271→376, plus three known). That is the measured case **for** the
embedding layer (decision B), which stays deferred; this loop did not reopen it.

**Deliberately not tuned.** Tuning the ranker to 70 cases authored in the same
session would launder the eval into a scoreboard. Measured, recorded, stopped.
